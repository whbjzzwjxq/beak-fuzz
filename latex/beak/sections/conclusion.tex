\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

We presented \tool, a semantics-guided differential fuzzing framework for discovering soundness bugs in zero-knowledge virtual machines. \tool introduces two contributions. First, a \emph{cross-zkVM semantic model} that unifies six heterogeneous RISC-V zkVM backends into a common space of 17 observation types and 32 semantic categories, together with a formal characterization of the two structurally distinct bug classes (Type~I divergences and Type~II underconstraints) and a two-phase coverage characterization establishing that both detection mechanisms are necessary and complementary. Second, a \emph{boundary-driven fuzzing framework} that uses category signature novelty as feedback, ISA-aware bandit-guided mutation for input generation, and semantic witness injection for detecting underconstrained circuits.

Evaluated across OpenVM, SP1, RISC Zero, Jolt, Nexus, and Pico on a benchmark of 44 known vulnerabilities, \tool achieves 81.8\% recall (1.6$\times$ over the state-of-the-art on comparable backends, 3.3$\times$ across all six) and discovers 6 previously unknown zero-day vulnerabilities, including critical soundness bugs confirmed and patched by the respective development teams. The formal framework provides a structural explanation for the performance gap: 86\% of detected bugs are Type~II (underconstrained witnesses), invisible to any purely differential approach.

Three broader findings emerge from the evaluation. First, verifier-side bugs outnumber prover-side bugs by a 31:13 ratio, establishing underconstraint as the dominant vulnerability class in production zkVMs---a result with direct implications for testing methodology. Second, 97\% of semantic categories activate across six architecturally diverse backends, demonstrating that constraint-correctness concerns are remarkably uniform despite implementation heterogeneity. Third, the independent emergence of the same x0 register vulnerability in three unrelated codebases suggests that certain ISA-level invariants create systematic blind spots in constraint-system development.

The 8 undetected bugs delineate a clear limitation: they require cross-component algebraic reasoning (interaction-digest collisions, field-modulus wraparound, witness canonicity violations) that goes beyond single-instruction boundary conditions. Two directions for future work are particularly promising: extending the observation model with interaction-level categories to capture cross-chip protocol invariants, and integrating lightweight formal reasoning about field-arithmetic properties to detect modular-arithmetic bugs. More broadly, the semantic model is extensible beyond RISC-V: adapting \tool to MIPS-based or WASM-based zkVM architectures requires defining corresponding observation types and categories, while the fuzzing framework and witness injection mechanisms remain unchanged. We will open-source \tool upon acceptance to support the growing need for automated security auditing in the zkVM ecosystem.
